The AI models people use every day learn a surprising amount from human experts. Behind every model that can explain a legal doctrine, check a dosage calculation or debug a tricky function, there are lawyers, physicians, scientists and engineers who taught it — and who keep testing it.

That work is called **AI training**, and it has become one of the most flexible ways for skilled professionals in the United States to earn extra income remotely.

## What does an AI trainer actually do?

AI training projects vary, but most involve a handful of core tasks:

- **Writing prompts and expert answers.** You create realistic, challenging questions in your field and write the answer an expert would give.
- **Evaluating AI responses.** You compare and rank model outputs for accuracy, reasoning, clarity and safety.
- **Explaining mistakes.** When a model gets something wrong, you explain exactly where and why — this feedback is what improves the next version.
- **Writing step-by-step solutions.** In math, physics, chemistry and finance, models learn to reason from worked solutions.
- **Reviewing code.** Software engineers write and review code, tests and explanations for coding-focused models.

You may hear this work described as RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback), expert data annotation or AI tutoring.

## Which fields are in demand?

Almost any field where deep knowledge matters. Common areas include:

- Law and legal research
- Medicine, nursing and pharmacy
- Physics, chemistry and biology
- Mathematics and statistics
- Finance, accounting and economics
- Software engineering and data science
- Writing, languages and the humanities

If you're genuinely talented at something, there's a good chance an AI project needs that knowledge.

## Do you need AI or coding experience?

No. Your expertise in your own field is what matters most. Platforms provide guidelines and tools, and most of the work happens in a simple web interface. Coding is only required for software engineering projects.

What *does* matter:

- Clear, precise written English
- Strong attention to detail and the patience to follow detailed guidelines
- The ability to explain your reasoning, not just give answers

## How the AI training ecosystem works

AI labs usually don't hire trainers one by one. They work through specialized AI training platforms — names you may have seen include Mercor, micro1, DataAnnotation, AfterQuery, Handshake, Snorkel AI, Alignerr and Outlier. Each platform runs different projects with their own assessments, schedules and pay rates.

## How much can you earn?

AI training is paid hourly in US dollars. Rates depend on your field, qualifications and the project. Highly specialized experts — physicians, attorneys, PhD scientists and senior engineers — generally earn the highest rates. Because most projects let you choose your hours, it works well alongside a full-time job or studies.
