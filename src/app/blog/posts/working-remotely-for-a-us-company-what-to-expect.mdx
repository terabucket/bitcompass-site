Landing a remote job with a US company can change your career — and your finances. But it also comes with a few differences from working for a local employer. Here's what international developers should know before accepting an offer.

## How you're usually hired

Most international developers work with US companies in one of two ways:

- **As an independent contractor.** You invoice monthly or bi-weekly and are responsible for your own local taxes and social contributions. This is the most common setup.
- **Through an Employer of Record (EOR).** A third-party company employs you locally on the US company's behalf, handling payroll and local benefits.

Either way, read your contract carefully and ask questions before signing. At BitCompass we walk every candidate through their contract terms before they accept. For tax questions specific to your country, talk to a local accountant.

## Getting paid in US dollars

Payments are usually made in USD through international bank transfer or a payment platform. Ask in advance how often you'll be paid, which method will be used and who covers transfer fees, so there are no surprises.

## Your working hours

Expect to overlap at least four hours with your team's US working day. Many teams are spread from Eastern Time (ET) to Pacific Time (PT), so agree on your core hours early and keep them consistent. Predictability builds trust.

## Communication is part of the job

US remote teams run on clear, written communication:

- Post daily updates on what you did, what's next and what's blocking you.
- Ask questions early instead of staying stuck for hours.
- Write descriptive pull requests and keep tickets up to date.
- Turn your camera on for meetings when you can — it helps build relationships.

Being proactive and visible matters as much as writing good code.

## Tools you'll likely use

Slack or Microsoft Teams for chat, Zoom or Google Meet for calls, GitHub or GitLab for code, Jira or Linear for tickets, and Notion or Confluence for documentation. If you haven't used them before, a quick tutorial goes a long way.

## Time off and holidays

Paid time off and holidays are agreed with each company. US holidays such as Thanksgiving and July 4th often mean quieter weeks; your own national holidays are usually respected if you communicate them in advance.
