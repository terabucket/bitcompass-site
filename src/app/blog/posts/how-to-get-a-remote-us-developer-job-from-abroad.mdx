Thousands of US companies now hire software engineers who live outside the United States. You don't need a visa, you don't need to relocate and, for many roles, you don't even need to have worked with an American company before. What you do need is the right preparation — and the right introductions.

Here's the process we walk our candidates through at BitCompass.

## 1. Understand what US companies are really hiring for

US teams hiring remotely from abroad usually look for three things:

- **Strong, current technical skills** in a mainstream stack such as React, Next.js, Node.js, TypeScript, Python or cloud platforms like AWS.
- **Clear communication** in English — spoken in stand-ups and written in pull requests, tickets and Slack.
- **Reliability and ownership.** Remote teams need people who deliver without constant supervision.

If you can show all three, where you live matters much less than you think.

## 2. Choose a focused role and stack

"Developer who can do anything" is hard to place. "Full-stack developer with four years of React, Next.js and Node.js, plus PostgreSQL and AWS" is easy to match with a specific job. Pick the role you're strongest in and present yourself clearly for it.

Full-stack web development is one of the most in-demand remote roles because one engineer can ship a whole feature end to end. If that's you, take a look at our open [Full-Stack Web Developer role](/jobs/full-stack-web-developer).

## 3. Make your resume US-ready

American recruiters skim. Make their job easy:

- Keep it to **one or two pages**, in English, as a PDF.
- Lead with a short summary: your role, years of experience and core stack.
- Write achievements, not duties — "Cut page load time by 40% by migrating to Next.js" beats "Worked on the frontend".
- Leave out photos, date of birth and marital status; US resumes don't include them.
- Add links to your LinkedIn, GitHub or portfolio.

We cover this in detail in our guide to [writing a resume for US remote jobs](/blog/full-stack-developer-resume-tips-for-us-remote-jobs).

## 4. Work on your time-zone overlap

Most US teams expect at least four hours of overlap with their working day. Developers in Latin America often overlap naturally; engineers in Europe, Africa and Asia usually shift their schedule to afternoons and evenings. Decide in advance what hours you can commit to — you'll be asked in every interview.

## 5. Practice the US interview style

A typical process includes a recruiter call, a technical interview (live coding or a take-home task), a system design or architecture conversation for senior roles, and a culture-fit interview with the hiring manager. Practice explaining your reasoning out loud in English — that matters as much as getting the right answer.

## 6. Get introduced, don't just apply

Cold applications to US job boards compete with hundreds of other candidates. A talent agency like BitCompass works differently: we vet you once, prepare you, and then introduce you directly to US companies that are actively hiring for your profile. Hiring managers trust a vetted introduction far more than a cold resume.
